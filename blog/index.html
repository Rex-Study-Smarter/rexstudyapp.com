Rex

Why I built Rex: a whiteboard with a tutor on it

Chatbots made answers free. That broke studying, not fixed it.

The problem I kept seeing

A student pastes a problem into a chatbot, reads the answer, nods along — and by morning it's gone. I watched it happen for months before I built anything. The answer was never the hard part. The hard part is the step where your own reasoning breaks, and nothing in a chat window looks for that step. It just hands you the next answer.

What Rex is

Rex is a study whiteboard for iPhone, iPad and Mac. You put a subject on an infinite board — notes, PDFs, your own handwriting, interactive charts, 3D science scenes you can walk through — and a tutor walks the material with you, out loud.

The tutor has one rule: it doesn't hand you answers. It asks you to attempt a step first, finds the exact step where your attempt went sideways, and makes you redo that step. Like a good human tutor, it's interested in your thinking, not your answer.

The parts students actually keep using

  • Boards that become flashcards. Anything on a board can turn into a deck on a spaced-repetition schedule, and into a quiz when exam week arrives.
  • The Rexer. A board becomes a narrated lecture — two hosts discussing your notes, with recall questions in the gaps. Your commute becomes revision.
  • A solver that shows its working. Exact algebra and calculus, line by line, running on your device. It works with no connection at all.
  • Voice, on device. Talk through a problem hands-free. Your speech never leaves the phone.
  • A chart library. Sixty-plus interactive widgets — a beating heart, sankey diagrams, case drills — that you can build, publish and remix.

Try a chart without installing anything

A few of them now run right in your browser. Watch what a 3×3 matrix does to space, build a cross product step by step, or drill Russian cases with the Case Compass. No account, no install.
